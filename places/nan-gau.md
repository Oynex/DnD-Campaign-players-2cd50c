## Overview
The magnificent imperial capital of the **[[celestial-empire-of-grand-cathay]]** and the **first recorded mortal settlement in the history of [[rubicon]]**. 

Built over the sacred battleground where the primordial Dragon Emperor defeated his greatest rival at the dawn of the world, Nan-Gau is a sprawling metropolis of tiered jade pagodas, serpentine waterways, and celestial arches where mortals, spirits, and fey live in sacred harmony.

> [!read-aloud]
> Soaring pagodas of green jade and crimson lacquer rise above the mist where four great rivers diverge. In the open plazas and river bridges, humans, fey creatures, and playful nature spirits mingle freely beneath the shadows of serpentine flying dragons that glide through the clouds without wings.

## The Primordial Duel & The Four Serpentine Rivers
Back when the world was still maturing—when High Elves lived in untamed woods and humans were wandering primitive tribes—the two mightiest primordial serpentine dragons fought a cataclysmic duel that reshaped the geography of eastern Rubicon:
- Their final showdown occurred where Nan-Gau stands today, shattering the continental bedrock.
- From that impact point, **four colossal rivers flow in four directions**, carving serpentine curves across the empire into the outer seas.
- Following his victory, the Dragon Emperor took human form, gathered the primitive tribes who worshipped him, and founded Nan-Gau as the cradle of civilization.

## The Western Frontier: The Rune Desert
Directly west of the river-fed green plains of Nan-Gau lie the desolate white dunes of **[[the-rune-desert]]**:
- **Echo of Cataclysm:** Five thousand years ago, the over-exploitation of **[[runic-magic-crafting]]** and massive magical crystal disasters sapped the western lands of all ambient mana, creating a vast dead wasteland.
- **The Imperial Edict:** Following the disaster, the Emperor banned all uncertified runic magic for the general populace, establishing the **[[guild-of-imperial-runesmiths]]** in Nan-Gau to keep the ancient art strictly regulated, alive, and in service to the throne.
