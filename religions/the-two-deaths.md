## Overview
The ancient shamanistic faith of the **Orcs of [[durotar]]**, practiced for untold centuries before the blood-red banners of **[[noxus]]** subjugated the red clay canyons of eastern [[aethelgard]]. Known among orc clans simply as the **Way of the Two Deaths** (or *Grom-Karash*).

Unlike continental religions built on golden cathedrals, church taxes, or promises of heavenly paradise, the orcish faith is rooted in an unyielding, melancholic truth of mortality:
> *"All mortals die twice. The first death occurs when the body fails and the breath leaves the lungs. The second death occurs when the last living person forgets their name. To remember is an act of supreme devotion; to be remembered is the only true immortality."*

> [!read-aloud]
> Beneath the blood-red sandstone hoodoos of Durotar, an elder orc shaman beats an elk-hide drum over a roaring pit of aromatic mesquite coals. In a guttural, harmonic chant that rattles the stone canyons, he recites the names, deeds, and battle-deaths of four hundred ancestors stretching back twelve generations. Around the fire, young tusks sit cross-legged in reverent silence, committing every syllable to memory so the fallen will not suffer the Second Death.

## The Two Deaths
- **The First Death (The Shattered Vessel):** When an orc falls in battle, dies of sickness, or succumbs to age, their physical flesh is returned to the red earth through funeral pyres. This death is natural, honorable, and expected—a warrior does not weep for the broken clay.
- **The Second Death (The Eternal Void):** The true horror of orcish spiritual life is being forgotten. When an orc’s name is spoken for the last time by mortal lips, their spirit slips into the cold, silent void beyond existence, severed forever from the ancestors. To let an ancestor’s name die is considered a bloodline crime punishable by spiritual banishment.

## Shamanic Rites & The Name Keepers
- **The Name-Singers (*Drak-Thun*):** Orcish shamans are not priests who preach divine commandments; they are living libraries of memory. A shaman must memorize thousands of names, family lineages, and the exact dying words of fallen warriors.
- **The Ash-Marking:** When an orc takes their first kill or builds their first forge, their body is tattooed with iron-and-ash ink bearing the runes of three ancestors whom they must keep alive in memory through their own honorable deeds.
- **Battlefield Invocation:** Before charging into slaughter, orc warriors do not pray to gods for protection. Instead, they roar their own names and the names of their grandfathers into the wind, defying the enemy to erase them from memory.

## The Secret Resistance Under Noxus
When **[[darius|Grand General Darius]]** and the armies of **[[noxus]]** conquered Durotar, imperial commissars banned public ancestral drumming, seeking to replace orcish tribal loyalty with total devotion to the Noxian war machine:
- **Imperial Desecration:** Noxus demands that fallen orcs be buried in mass unmarked legionary trenches, an imperial efficiency that the shamans view as a deliberate, horrific attempt to inflict the Second Death on entire generations.
- **Clandestine Cairns:** Behind the backs of Noxian centurions, orc legionnaires whisper the names of their fallen comrades into small iron tokens, burying them beneath secret trail cairns in the dead of night. Even as frontline shock troops for the empire, their hearts belong to the Unforgotten.
