## Overview
The fiercely independent, reformation faith of the **[[skyrim|Northern Jarldom of Skyrim]]**, embraced by jarls, mountain clansmen, and coastal shield-warriors. Its followers proudly call themselves the **New Malors** (or the *Hearth-Brethren*).

While sharing historical roots with the ancient southern faith of **[[orthodox-malorism]]**, the New Malors arose two millennia ago as a radical spiritual revolution against what northerners perceived as the total corruption, greed, and idolatry of the church in the **[[holy-city-of-vivec]]**. They hold that southern priests locked the Prophet in a cage of gold, and that true communion with Malor exists only around the humble, shared fire of the hearth.

> [!read-aloud]
> Within a rough-hewn timber longhouse, a circle of weathered clansmen, shield-maidens, and gray-bearded elders gather around a roaring stone hearth. No priest in spun gold leads the rites; instead, a scarred skald strikes an iron chord upon a battered lute, reciting the verses of Prophet Malor’s winter trials. Bread and roasted venison are passed freely hand to hand, every warrior bowing their head toward the embers in solemn, silent gratitude.

## Born in the Deep North: The Hearth's Claim
To the New Malors, Prophet Malor is not a distant imperial icon, but a son of the northern crags:
- **Son of the Frost:** Malor was born in the deep north of Skyrim, surviving brutal blizzards as a youth and learning the values of clan loyalty, endurance, and shared warmth around a timber fire.
- **The Stolen Prophet:** When Malor journeyed south across Tamriel to bring his light to all peoples, the New Malors believe that the southern kings and bishops corrupted his teachings after his passing. They built stone palaces and demanded coin in his name, perverting a northern holy wanderer into an excuse for imperial taxes.
- **The Barefoot Wanderer:** The New Malors venerate Prophet Malor as he was: the barefoot, blistered pilgrim who slept in the dirt, healed the destitute, and forgave the bandits who beat him. Their foundational creed is drawn from his farewell sermon to his northern kin: *"The divine dwells not within carved granite, nor in the coffers of kings, but in the heat of the fire shared between brothers freezing in the dark."*

## The Northern Reformation: Stripping the Gold
- **Rejection of the Tithe:** The New Malors view mandatory church taxes and the sale of holy pardons as supreme blasphemy—selling God's grace for silver. In the North, spiritual aid is freely given; any man who demands coin for a prayer is driven out into the snow.
- **An Unbroken Circle:** There are no bishops, popes, or stone cathedrals among the New Malors. Spiritual guidance falls naturally to village elders, clan wise-women, and wandering skald-friars who travel barefoot between mountain steadings without coin, tending the sick and reciting the holy tales.
- **Churches of Malor in the North:** While fortified **Churches of Malor** staffed by **[[the-knights-of-malor]]** exist in southern Cyrodiil, northern holds fiercely forbid their construction. In Skyrim, a "church" is simply the clan longhouse or a sacred circle of standing stones carved with Malor’s trial runes.

## Revered Feats of Prophet Malor in the North
While southern cathedrals emphasize Malor's miracles of water and divine kingship, the New Malors sing of his raw physical endurance and moral defiance:
- **The Winter Fast on the Throat of the World:** Northern skalds tell of how Malor scaled the frozen precipices of Tamriel's highest mountain during the Great Ice Winter, praying without cloak or fire for seven weeks until the mountain frost spirits bowed in respect, acknowledging him as the Brother of Frost.
- **The Breaking of the Bandit's Blade:** When cornered by murderous outlaws, he disarmed them not with sorcery or blades, but by extending an open hand and offering his own bread. The New Malors cite this as proof that steel cannot conquer a soul grounded in truth.
- **Defiance of the Tyrant:** They revere his imprisonment under King Vaelen as proof that no righteous follower of Malor should ever bow to kings, high canons, or taxes.
- **The Pestilence Trenches:** They recall how Malor knelt in the mud beside dying peasants when the local lords fled in their carriages, proving that God walks with the abandoned, not the powerful.

## Rituals and Daily Practice
- **The Hearth Rite:** Every meal begins with a pinch of salt and rendered animal fat cast into the central hearth, giving thanks to the divine for granting life and warmth amidst the unforgiving frost.
- **The Stone of Passage:** Coming-of-age youths make a solitary pilgrimage into the blizzard crags, returning with a carved river-stone to prove they possess the fortitude of Prophet Malor.
- **Holy Retribution:** To the New Malors, the gold-plated ziggurats of Vivec and the marching columns of **[[the-knights-of-malor]]** are an insult to the Prophet's memory. When Nordic raiding parties sweep down upon southern coasts, ripping gold leaf from imperial temples is considered a righteous holy act: reclaiming the Prophet’s name from corrupt southern priests.
