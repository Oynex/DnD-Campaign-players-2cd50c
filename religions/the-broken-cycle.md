## Overview
The clandestine, rapidly metastasizing urban faith spreading among the hundreds of thousands of disenfranchised factory hands, crystal smelters, and sewer miners in the subterranean industrial underbelly of **[[piltover]]**. Known simply to its adherents as **The Broken Cycle** (or *The Free Cogs*).

Born in a city-state that pridefully claims to have outgrown the need for religion, The Broken Cycle is not an ancient scripture, but a raw, desperate mythology birthed from the soot, toxic sulfuric fumes, and industrial brutality of the crystal boom. To the crushed lower classes, the tri-cornered tyranny of Piltover’s **[[council-of-piltover|Five Corporate Cartels]]**, aristocratic noble houses, and chem-baron syndicates is a grinding iron machine—and their faith promises that one day, the Great Lever will be pulled to smash the machine to dust.

> [!read-aloud]
> Deep within the dripping, rust-streaked drainage tunnels beneath the factory spires, hundreds of soot-stained miners and crystal polishers gather in candle-lit gloom. Many nurse chemical coughs or wear patched leather masks over scarred faces. At the center of the tunnel, atop a rusty turbine casing, an orator holds aloft a severed brass gear split cleanly in two. In hushed, fervent unison, the crowd taps heavy iron wrenches against the stone floor in a rhythmic, mechanical pulse: *"The cog shall turn no more. The wheel shall break."*

## The Legend of the First Mechanic & The Broken Cycle
Piltover’s upper classes scoff at religion, celebrating cold calculation, profit margins, and [[magitech]]. But down in the sumps, myth has taken root:
- **The Myth of the Great Engine:** The faith teaches that mortal society was not designed by benevolent deities, but forged as a predatory clockwork engine by ancient tyrants to siphon the vital life-force of the working masses.
- **The Lie of Progress:** While corporate magnates proclaim that magitech brings prosperity to all, the faithful teach that every crystal refined and every airship launched merely tightens the golden chains around the necks of their children.
- **Breaking the Cycle:** The core prophecy foretells the coming of the **Great Lever**—a subterranean revolutionary upheaval or arcane cataclysm triggered from the glowing red crater basin that will shatter the corporate balance, free the human cogs from the machinery, and break the cycle of generational exploitation forever.

## Rituals and Symbology
- **The Split Gear:** Followers carry small, cracked brass cogs concealed beneath their work aprons or tattooed onto the inside of their wrists. Showing the split gear is a sign of mutual sanctuary and worker solidarity.
- **The Shift vigil:** Rather than Sabbath days, rites are held during the changing of the fourteen-hour factory shifts in damp tunnels beneath the crystal vats.
- **The Tithe of Steel:** Adherents do not donate silver; they quietly steal small valves, firing pins, and crystal capacitors from factory assembly lines, burying them in hidden subterranean armories in preparation for the "Day of the Uncoupling."

## Threat to the Tri-Cornered Power
The rapid rise of The Broken Cycle terrifies Piltover’s elite:
- The **Corporate Council** labels it dangerous syndicalist treason, deploying armed private Pinkerton enforcers to raid tunnel assemblies.
- The **Old Noble Houses** dismiss it as degenerate peasant hysteria, oblivious to how deeply their own scullery servants and coachmen have embraced the creed.
- The **Underground Crime Barons** view the cult with volatile suspicion; while some chem-lords sell black-market arms to the movement, they fear a worker uprising would incinerate their illegal distribution networks along with the factories.
